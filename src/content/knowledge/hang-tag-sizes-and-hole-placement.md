Hang tag sizes are usually chosen from a small set of working formats, then adjusted for the product. The format matters because a tag that is too large looks like a luggage label, and one that is too small cannot carry the brand, the price and the mandatory text without turning into a wall of 5 pt type.

## Working sizes

| Format | Size (mm) | Typical use |
|---|---:|---|
| Small | 50 × 90 | Jewellery, small accessories, sample sachets |
| Standard | 55 × 90 | Apparel, socks, accessories |
| Large | 60 × 100 | Candles, home fragrance, gift sets |
| Landscape | 90 × 50 | Short brand statements, care tags |
| Statement | 100 × 150 | Press kits, limited editions, display tags |

These are starting points. A 55 × 90 mm tag is common because it fits a standard retail rail and leaves room for a logo, a product name and a small barcode. A 60 × 100 mm tag gives more room for warning text or a story, but it can overwhelm a small product.

If you are working in inches, convert at 25.4 mm per inch. A 2 × 3.5 in tag is 51 × 89 mm; a 2.25 × 3.5 in tag is 57 × 89 mm. The nearest working format is usually close enough, but do not convert a size and then round it twice.

## The hole is a structural decision

The hole does three things: it locates the string, it takes the load, and it tells the tag which way is up. A centred hole makes the tag hang straight; a hole that is 3 mm off-centre makes even a stiff tag hang at an angle.

The usual position is 8–10 mm from the top edge, centred left to right. Leave about 3 mm of clear space around the hole. On a 350 gsm card, a 4 mm hole without an eyelet can ovalise after a few weeks of handling; an eyelet spreads the load and protects the card. On a 400 gsm card, the hole needs a little more clearance because the board is less forgiving at a tight radius.

If the tag has a folded top or a reinforced header, the hole position changes. Decide the construction before the dieline: a folded header doubles the card at the hole, which can be stronger but also bulkier.

## String, knot and finished drop

The string adds length above the tag. A simple knot can add 10–15 mm; a loop or a bow can add more. If the buyer specifies a finished drop from the rail to the bottom of the tag, work backwards: finished drop − tag height − knot allowance = string length.

The attachment also affects the hole. A thin cotton string cuts into an unprotected card; a waxed cord slides more easily; elastic stretches and can pull the tag against the product. A safety pin avoids the hole entirely but changes the look. Choose the attachment before the artwork so the hole, eyelet and string channel are sized for it.

## Front and back

A hang tag is often printed both sides. The front carries the brand and the product; the back can carry care instructions, material, price, barcode, batch code or a QR link. If the back has a lot of text, the tag may need to be larger or the text may need a folded insert.

For retail, the barcode needs a quiet zone and enough magnification. A small tag can force the barcode below a comfortable size; if the barcode is mandatory, size the tag around it rather than shrinking the symbol. A QR code is more forgiving but still needs contrast and a quiet zone.

## Common mistakes

**Sizing the tag before the text.** A tag that looks generous in a mock-up can be tight once the care text, price and barcode are placed. Start with the text block.

**Putting the hole too close to the edge.** A hole 3–4 mm from the top can tear out, especially without an eyelet. Keep 8–10 mm and add an eyelet for handling.

**Forgetting the knot.** A tag that is the right size can hang too low because the string knot was not allowed for. Specify the finished drop, not just the card size.

**Using a landscape format for a rail.** A landscape tag can look distinctive, but it may not sit well on a standard hook or may hide the product. Check the retail fixture before choosing.

## What this means for buyers

**Start with the fixture and the text.** The rail, the product and the mandatory information decide the size more than the design does.

**Centre the hole and allow for the knot.** A straight-hanging tag looks more expensive than a larger tag that hangs crooked.

**Add an eyelet if the tag will be handled.** It is a small cost against a torn hole and a lost tag.

**Keep the barcode at a readable size.** If it does not fit, change the tag format rather than shrinking the symbol below the scanner’s comfort zone.
