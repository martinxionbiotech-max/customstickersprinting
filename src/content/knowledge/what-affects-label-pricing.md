Label pricing looks opaque because five variables move at once. In practice, each one behaves predictably, and knowing which to change is how a quote gets cheaper without losing quality.

## The five variables

| Variable | Effect on price | Lever |
|---|---|---|
| Printed area | Roughly proportional to material use | Smaller label, less text |
| Material | Paper < PP < PET < speciality | Match to condition, not to habit |
| Finishing | Laminate, foil, spot UV add passes | Skip laminate indoors |
| Quantity | Setup amortised over more units | Combine designs, reorder |
| Cutting | Simple shape < custom die | Circle instead of contour |

## Area

Material and ink scale with printed area. Doubling the area roughly doubles material cost but barely changes setup — which is why small labels at high volume are cheap and large labels at low volume are expensive.

If a label is 20% larger than it needs to be, that is 20% more material on every unit for the life of the product.

## Material

Paper is cheapest, PP is mid, PET and speciality films are higher. The mistake is choosing paper for a chilled product to save 20% — the reprint costs more than the saving.

The right question is not "what is the cheapest material" but "what is the cheapest material that survives the application".

## Finishing

Lamination, foil and spot UV are extra passes. Each adds cost and, in the case of foil, a die.

Lamination is the one finish that is often a false economy to skip: it protects the print, and on a handled product its absence shows within weeks.

## Quantity

Setup is fixed. At 50 units it is most of the price; at 5,000 it is a rounding error. This is why the per-unit price curve is steep at the start and flat later.

Combining designs into one order shares the setup where material and size match — the most reliable way to reduce a first order's unit price.

## Cutting

A circle or rounded rectangle uses a standard die or a plotter path. A custom contour needs a die above a few hundred units, which is a one-off cost that never disappears.

At 50 units, a simple shape can be half the price of a complex contour.

## What does not affect price much

- **Number of colours** in digital printing (unlike flexo, where each colour is a plate).
- **Artwork complexity**, unless it requires manual prep.
- **Delivery speed**, unless you pay for express production.

## What this means for buyers

**Change the shape before changing the material.** A simpler cut often saves more than downgrading the stock — and does not risk the application.

**Combine designs to share setup.** Three 50-unit designs in one order cost less than three separate orders.

**Do not optimise the unit price on the first run.** The goal of the first order is to validate; the second order is where volume pricing applies.

**Ask for the price breakdown.** A quote that separates setup, material and finishing shows which lever to pull.
