Stickers fail at the adhesive far more often than at the print. The choice is narrower than for labels, because stickers go on unpredictable surfaces — a laptop, a window, a painted wall, a shipping box.

## The three adhesives

**Permanent acrylic** is the default: strong, durable, water-resistant, and effectively not removable without residue.

**Removable acrylic** peels cleanly for weeks or months on smooth surfaces. It is not reusable, and "removable" is a duration, not a promise.

**Outdoor-grade acrylic** adds UV and temperature resistance for glass, vehicles and equipment.

## Surface matching

| Surface | Adhesive | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Glass | Permanent or outdoor | Clean first; dust shows through clear film |
| Laptop lid (anodised aluminium) | Permanent | Textured finishes reduce contact area |
| HDPE / PP plastic | LSE adhesive | Standard glue slides |
| Powder-coated metal | LSE, test first | Texture traps air |
| Painted wall | Removable | Permanent pulls paint |
| Kraft board | Permanent | Porous; bonds fast |
| Car window | Outdoor-grade | Interior stickers fade differently |

## Removable, honestly

Removable stickers are used on retail packaging, returnable crates and promotional surfaces. Two caveats:

- **Time matters.** A removable label left in sun for a year may leave residue.
- **Surface matters more.** Removable on glass is clean; on cheap paint it can lift the paint.

If clean removal is the requirement, say the surface and the duration — the adhesive follows from those two facts.

## Clear film and adhesive

Clear stickers show everything, including the adhesive's behaviour: a slightly uneven bond reads as a bubble. Clean the surface, apply with a squeegee, and expect clear film to be less forgiving than white.

## A surface test you can run before the full order

Adhesive data sheets describe a standard test surface, not your laptop, powder-coated panel or recycled bottle. A practical test takes a week and costs one sample:

1. Clean the surface the way the product will be cleaned, then let it dry completely.
2. Apply the sticker by hand, pressing from the centre out with a squeegee or a clean cloth.
3. Leave it at room temperature for 24 hours; initial tack is not final adhesion.
4. Check the edges at 24 hours, one week and one month.
5. Put one sample in the conditions the product will see: a bathroom, a fridge, a car window or a wet outdoor surface.
6. Record where it lifts, whether the adhesive transfers to the surface, and whether the print changes.

A sticker that passes on a glass jar may fail on a powder-coated tin because the texture reduces contact area. A sticker that holds on a laptop may slide on a recycled HDPE bottle because the surface energy is lower. The test is not a guarantee for every batch, but it catches the obvious mismatch before a production run.

If removal matters, test that too. A removable adhesive can peel cleanly from glass and leave residue on cheap paint; the surface, not the word “removable”, decides.

## What this means for buyers

**Permanent is the right default for packaging and products.** Removable is for surfaces the customer owns.

**Name the surface in the brief.** "Sticker for a bottle" is not enough — glass, PET and HDPE differ.

**Outdoor is a separate specification.** UV-stable film plus laminate plus outdoor adhesive; one of the three missing shortens life dramatically.

**Ask for a sample on the actual surface.** A sticker that holds on a desk may slide on a powder-coated panel.

## The edge lift that starts before the customer applies the sticker

A sticker can feel securely bonded in the hand and still lift at one edge a day later. The adhesive needs time to wet the surface, and that contact is easily interrupted by dust, oil, a release liner that has curled in storage, or pressure applied only to the centre. On a textured coating, the glue may touch the peaks while leaving air in the valleys. The print can be perfect while the bond is only partial.

The correct adhesive depends on the surface and the application routine. Glass is forgiving; HDPE, recycled plastic and powder-coated metal are less so. A cold label or cold container slows the initial bond, and a curved sticker needs pressure carried around the full perimeter rather than across the middle. Removable adhesive behaves differently again: it may hold in a showroom and release in a warm car. Storage instructions are part of the specification, not an afterthought.

Application guidance belongs with the [custom stickers](/custom-stickers/) order: cleaning method, surface temperature, pressure and how long to leave the sticker before handling. Apply a sample to the production surface, burnish the edges and inspect again after normal handling.
