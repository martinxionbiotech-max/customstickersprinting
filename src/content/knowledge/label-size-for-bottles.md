Bottle labelling fails at the two ends: too tall and it creases into the shoulder radius, too wide and it runs past the point where the curve tightens. The volume printed on the bottle does not tell you either dimension — the diameter and the body height do.

This page gives typical sizes by volume, and the reason they are only typical. For the measurement method itself, see the [label size guide](/knowledge/label-size-guide/).

## Why volume is a poor guide

A 250 ml bottle can be short and wide or tall and narrow. Both hold the same liquid, and both need a different label:

| Shape | Diameter | Body height | Label |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Short, wide 250 ml | 60 mm | 95 mm | 55 × 65 mm |
| Tall, narrow 250 ml | 45 mm | 150 mm | 42 × 95 mm |

Same volume, different label, same price bracket. This is why a quotation that asks only "what size bottle?" produces a label that fits the catalogue photo rather than the bottle on your desk.

## Typical sizes by volume

| Volume | Diameter | Body height | Front label | Full wrap |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| 30 ml dropper | 30 mm | 85 mm | 25 × 60 mm | 98 × 70 mm |
| 50 ml dropper | 33 mm | 95 mm | 30 × 70 mm | 108 × 75 mm |
| 100 ml | 40 mm | 110 mm | 40 × 80 mm | 130 × 90 mm |
| 250 ml | 50 mm | 130 mm | 50 × 90 mm | 161 × 110 mm |
| 330 ml beverage | 60 mm | 115 mm | 60 × 90 mm | 192 × 100 mm |
| 500 ml | 65 mm | 200 mm | 65 × 120 mm | 208 × 150 mm |
| 750 ml wine | 75 mm | 290 mm | 90 × 120 mm | 240 × 100 mm |
| 1 litre | 80 mm | 230 mm | 80 × 130 mm | 255 × 150 mm |

Wrap widths in the last column already include 4 mm of overlap. For the arithmetic, see [calculating a wrap label](/knowledge/label-wrap-calculation/).

## How much of the bottle to cover

Coverage is a design decision with a practical consequence: the more of the bottle the label covers, the less room there is to see the product, and the more room there is for mandatory text.

| Coverage | Look | Where it works |
|---|---|---|
| 30–40% of circumference | Premium, product visible | Spirits, serums, oils |
| 45–60% | Standard front panel | Skincare, sauces, beverages |
| 70–90% | Information-led | Food with ingredients and nutrition |
| Full wrap | Container becomes the label | Candles, cans, some beverages |

If the label has to carry ingredients, allergens, a barcode and an address, work backwards from the text rather than forwards from the design. A 50 × 90 mm label holds roughly 250 words at 7 pt; a 65 × 120 mm holds around 450.

## Neck labels and shoulder seals

Beverages and spirits often add a second small label at the neck. Typical sizes:

- Neck band for a wine bottle: 60 × 25 mm, or a wrap of 240 × 20 mm.
- Shoulder seal on a spirits bottle: a 25–35 mm circle.
- Safety seal over a cap: 30–40 mm circle, usually destructible stock.

Keep the neck label at least 10 mm below the cap so the closure does not tear it during capping. On a screw cap, a neck label placed too high is also the first thing to wrinkle when the bottle is opened.

## Bottles that need a different approach

**Tapered bottles** — a straight rectangle wrinkles at the wider end. Shorten the label or use a trapezoid dieline.

**Very small diameters** — below about 30 mm, film wants to lift at the edges. Use a thin face stock and test before a production run.

**Textured or frosted glass** — adhesive contacts only the peaks. Expect a lower bond and specify accordingly, especially on a chilled product.

**Recycled plastic** — batch variation in rHDPE changes surface energy. A label that passed on one batch can lift on the next, so test the actual bottles.

## What this means for buyers

**Send diameter and body height, not volume.** Those two numbers produce a correct quote; the volume produces a guess.

**Decide coverage before design.** It determines how much text fits, and retrofitting a legal panel into a premium-sized label is how designs get cramped.

**Add the neck label to the same order.** A separate small run for a second label often costs more than the label itself.

**Test the first size on the real bottle.** A 50-unit sample order is the cheapest way to find out that a 60 mm label crowds a 65 mm body.
