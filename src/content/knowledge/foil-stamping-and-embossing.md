Foil and embossing are the two finishes that change a label's physical surface rather than its ink. Both need a die, both work best on certain stocks, and both fail visibly when specified at the wrong quantity.

## Foil stamping

A heated die presses a metallic film onto the substrate, transferring a thin metal layer in the shape of the die.

**What it gives**: a mirror metallic — gold, silver, copper, holographic — that CMYK cannot reproduce.

**Constraints**:
- Needs a die, so it has a setup cost.
- Works best on smooth, coated stocks; on uncoated paper the foil can look pitted.
- Fine detail has a minimum stroke width (roughly 0.3 mm) below which the foil tears.
- Foil over dark ink can look muddy; foil on white or directly on the stock reads cleanest.

**Economics**: at 50 units the die dominates; from a few hundred upward it becomes a per-unit line item. It is usually combined with a base print, not used alone.

## Embossing and debossing

A matched die pair presses the material into relief (emboss) or into the surface (deboss).

**What it gives**: a tactile, raised or recessed element — a logo, a border, a pattern — that photographs poorly and feels expensive in the hand.

**Constraints**:
- Needs a matched male/female die pair, more expensive than a foil die.
- Requires a stock thick enough to hold the shape: paper at 120 gsm or above, or a laminated film.
- Thin films and small labels cannot hold fine emboss detail.
- Registration with print must be tight; misregistration shows as a shadow.

**Economics**: a premium technique with a premium setup — sensible on a hero product, rarely on a full range at low volume.

## Combining them

Foil plus emboss ("foil emboss") is common on spirits and premium cosmetics: the foil sits inside the raised area. It needs one combined die and careful proofing.

## What this means for buyers

**Foil is a colour problem; emboss is a texture problem.** If the goal is a metallic brand colour, foil; if the goal is tactile premium, emboss.

**Both need volume to pay off.** At 50 units, expect the die to be a meaningful share of the order — or choose a printed metallic effect instead.

**Stock matters.** Foil on uncoated paper looks distressed; emboss on thin film does not hold.

**Proof physically, not on screen.** Neither effect can be judged from a PDF; ask for a sample before the run.
