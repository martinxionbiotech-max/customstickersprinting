Finish is usually discussed as a look. It is also a durability decision, and on some products it decides whether the label survives its first week.

## What the finishes are

**Gloss** is a smooth, reflective laminate or varnish. Colours look saturated, blacks are deep, and the surface is easy to wipe.

**Matte** is a finely textured surface that diffuses light. Colours look flatter and more "premium"; contrast is lower.

**Soft-touch** is a velvety matte laminate with a rubberised feel. It photographs beautifully and marks easily.

**Uncoated paper** (no laminate) has a tactile, fibrous surface — the craft look — with the least water resistance.

## The trade-offs

| Finish | Colour | Fingerprints | Water | Scuff resistance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gloss | Saturated, high contrast | Shows | Good | Good |
| Matte | Muted, soft | Hides | Good | Very good |
| Soft-touch | Muted, premium | Shows badly | Good | Moderate |
| Uncoated | Flat, natural | Hides | Poor | Low |

The pattern: gloss shows handling, matte hides it, soft-touch shows everything, uncoated absorbs it.

## Matching finish to environment

- **Bathroom / cosmetics**: matte. It hides water spots and fingerprints that gloss exaggerates.
- **Chilled beverages**: gloss or matte laminate. Both handle condensation; gloss wipes clean.
- **Premium spirits**: uncoated textured paper or soft-touch, accepting that it will mark.
- **Outdoor stickers**: gloss laminate for colour and wipeability, matte for a subtler look with slightly better scuff resistance.
- **Retail cartons**: gloss for shelf pop, matte for a premium signal.

## The freezer problem

Lamination affects cold performance less than adhesive does, but a stiff gloss laminate on a label that goes from −18 °C to a warm kitchen can craze. On frozen products, specify a freezer-grade adhesive and a flexible laminate, and test the cycle.

## Cost

Lamination adds a pass. Matte and gloss cost roughly the same; soft-touch costs more; spot UV and foil are separate decorative passes quoted individually.

At 50 units, the finish is a small absolute cost. At 50,000, it is a line item worth negotiating.

## What this means for buyers

**Matte is the safer default for anything handled.** It hides the fingerprints that make a gloss label look tired in a shop.

**Do not use soft-touch on a product people touch with wet or oily hands.** It is beautiful in a photo and smudged in a bathroom.

**Finish does not fix a bad material choice.** A matte laminate on paper will still fail in a fridge; the film decides that, not the finish.

**Ask for a proof on the real substrate.** Gloss on white vinyl and gloss on clear film look like different products.
