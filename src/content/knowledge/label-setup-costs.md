A setup charge is the least popular line on a label quote and the easiest to misunderstand. It is not a markup added to small orders; it is the labour and tooling that happen before anything is printed, and it exists whether you order 50 labels or 50,000.

Part of the [cost and ordering guide](/knowledge/custom-label-printing-cost/).

## What is inside it

| Component | What happens | Scales with quantity? |
|---|---|---|
| File preparation | Bleed, resolution, fonts, cut path, white ink, colour conversion | No |
| Die | Steel rule die for a custom shape, or a plotter path | No |
| Proof | Digital proof, contract proof or physical sample | No |
| Make-ready | Mounting, registration, colour adjustment, start-up waste | No |

Four one-off tasks, one line on the invoice. On a digital press the make-ready is short; on flexo it includes plates and a longer registration, which is why flexo setup is several times larger.

## How the cost behaves

The share, not the amount, is what matters:

| Quantity | Setup per unit, at a $190 setup | Share of a $0.041 unit cost |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 50 | $3.80 | 99% |
| 250 | $0.76 | 95% |
| 1,000 | $0.19 | 82% |
| 5,000 | $0.04 | 48% |
| 20,000 | $0.01 | 19% |

The right-hand column is the one to read: it assumes the small label from the cost guide. A larger label with a $0.08 variable cost would show 33% at 5,000 units instead of 48% — the setup does not shrink, the label grows.

That arithmetic is the reason a low minimum exists at all. It is also the reason a supplier quoting 50 units cannot "just sharpen the price" — there is nothing left to sharpen except the parts of the setup that were optional.

## What is optional inside a setup

Not all of it is fixed. These parts can be reduced:

- **The die** — a standard size or a kiss-cut sheet removes it entirely.
- **The proof type** — a digital proof is cheap, a press proof is not. A physical sample sits between them.
- **File preparation** — a correct vector file with bleed and a cut path removes most of this hour.
- **Combining designs** — three designs that share a material and size share one setup.

What cannot be removed: make-ready. The press still needs to be set up for the first label, whatever the quantity.

## Paying twice: the three ways it happens

**1. A revision after approval.** Any change to the file after the proof resets the proof, and often the make-ready. Even a text edit. This is the most common cause of a second setup on a job that was quoted once.

**2. A die that was not kept.** Some suppliers discard dies after a run, or keep them for a limited period. On a reorder the die is made again. Ask how long the die is held — a year is reasonable for a repeat customer, and it should be in writing.

**3. A different supplier.** A new printer cannot reuse another's die or another's press profile, so a reorder elsewhere carries a full setup even if the file is identical.

The rule that follows: **freeze the artwork before approving, and reorder from the same supplier while the die exists.**

## Is the setup charge fair?

Question it in three ways, in this order:

1. **Ask what it includes.** If the answer is "setup" and nothing more, ask for the four components separately.
2. **Ask whether the die is retained** and for how long, in writing.
3. **Ask what would remove it** — a standard size, a kiss-cut sheet, a simpler cut path.

Those three questions distinguish a real setup from a padded one. A supplier who can answer them is charging for work; one who cannot is charging for the number.

## Setup on a reorder: what should change

| Item | First order | Reorder, identical spec |
|---|---|---|
| File preparation | Yes | No |
| Die | Yes, if custom | No, if retained |
| Proof | Yes | Optional, digital only |
| Make-ready | Yes | Yes |
| Material | Yes | Yes |

A reorder should therefore be cheaper **per unit at the same quantity**, not just cheaper in total. If it is not, ask which line changed.

## What this means for buyers

**Treat setup as a fixed block, not a negotiation.** Negotiating it usually means removing something you needed.

**Freeze the file before approval.** One revision costs more than the difference between two suppliers.

**Ask how long the die is kept.** It determines whether your second order pays it again.

**Prefer standard shapes at low volume.** It is the only way to remove a setup without removing quality.
