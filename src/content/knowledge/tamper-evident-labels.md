A tamper-evident label is a small piece of evidence. It can show that a carton flap was lifted, a bottle cap was turned or a closure was separated from its container. It cannot prove that the product inside is genuine, that the supply chain was secure at every step or that a determined person could not replace the whole package. The useful specification starts with the observation the buyer wants to make, not with a material called “security”.

This is production guidance, not legal advice. Some products are subject to specific tamper-evident packaging requirements; confirm those rules, the responsible party and the inspection process with the brand, importer or authority. See the [disclaimer](/disclaimer/). Part of the [specialty labels guide](/knowledge/specialty-labels/).

## Begin with the evidence, not the word “tamper”

Write a short scenario. Who opens the package, at which point, and what should they see if it has already been opened? A pharmacist inspecting a sealed carton needs a different result from a warehouse worker checking a pallet or a customer lifting a bottle cap. The label must be placed where the opening action disturbs it.

A label across a seam can be cut cleanly with a knife, lifted with heat or bypassed by opening another part of the pack. If the closure can be opened without touching the label, the construction is not providing evidence at that closure. Map the actual opening path before choosing the material.

The inspection standard should be visual and repeatable. “If the seal looks broken” is weaker than a defined reference sample showing an intact seal, a lifted edge and a peeled void message. Train the person who inspects the package to compare against that reference.

## Void labels: a message left behind

A void label is engineered so that peeling it transfers a visible message or pattern to the container. The intended effect is obvious interference: the label no longer sits flat, or the surface beneath it carries a “void” mark that cannot be mistaken for ordinary wear.

The result depends on the face stock, adhesive, release coating and container surface. A void construction that works on smooth glass may behave differently on a rough carton or a low-surface-energy plastic. The peel angle, speed and temperature also matter: a slow lift can leave a different mark from a fast pull.

Application pressure is part of the specification. A label that was not pressed evenly may lift at an edge before anyone opens the package, creating a false alarm. Ask for application instructions and test the same method the production line will use.

## Destructible vinyl and security slits

Destructible vinyl is designed to tear or fragment when someone tries to remove it. It can suit a seal over a carton, a closure or a small equipment panel where a transferred void message is not practical. The trade-off is handling: a face that tears easily under deliberate removal can also tear during packing, transport or normal use.

Security slits are cut patterns that interrupt the label when someone lifts it. They can make a clean peel more difficult and expose an attempt to move the label from one package to another. The pattern must be designed with the label shape and the opening direction; a slit that does not intersect the peel path may add visual complexity without improving evidence.

Tamper evidence can also use a fragile paper, a brittle film, a patterned adhesive or a combination of these. The material name alone does not define the result. Ask for a sample on the actual package and open it the way a customer or inspector would.

## The application surface decides whether the evidence is reliable

Glass and many rigid plastics are relatively forgiving. HDPE, PP, powder-coated metal and oily or dusty surfaces are not. A low-surface-energy container may need a high-tack or specially formulated adhesive, and the surface may need to be clean and dry before application.

Curved closures add peel force at the edges. A small-diameter cap can create a label that lifts by itself, while a flat carton gives the adhesive more contact. Test the label on the filled container, not an empty sample with a different surface finish.

Application temperature matters too. A label applied cold may not wet out fully, even if it performs well after it has warmed. Follow the material supplier's application guidance and test the actual line conditions. If the label is applied by hand, the pressure and dwell may differ from a machine application.

| Construction | Evidence it can leave | Main limit |
|---|---|---|
| Void label | A visible message or residue after peeling | Surface and peel conditions change the result |
| Destructible vinyl | A torn or fragmented face | Can tear during normal handling |
| Security slits | A broken pattern or interrupted face along the peel path | Pattern must intersect the actual opening route |
| Fragile paper seal | A split or torn seal across a closure | Moisture and abrasion can damage it before inspection |
| Patterned adhesive | A residue pattern on the container | Some surfaces release cleanly or absorb the residue |

## What the label proves, and what it does not

| The construction can support | The construction does not provide |
|---|---|
| A visible sign that a seal was disturbed | Proof that the product inside is genuine |
| A reference for a defined inspection point | A substitute for a controlled closure |
| Evidence of a lifted label on a tested surface | Protection against a copied label or replaced package |
| A consistent mark when the agreed peel method is used | A guarantee that every customer will inspect it |

Tamper evidence is usually one layer in a wider process. If authentication, serialisation or a regulated supply-chain requirement matters, the label should be designed alongside those controls rather than presented as a replacement for them.

## Removal residue and surface damage

The mark left behind is not always desirable. A void message on a premium glass bottle may be acceptable at inspection but unattractive to a collector. A destructible paper label may leave fibres that are difficult to remove from a reusable container. A strong adhesive may pull a coating or leave a tacky patch.

Decide whether the package is single-use, returnable or resold. If the label must be removable after inspection, test the removal method and the residue on the actual surface. A tamper-evident construction is designed not to separate cleanly; the buyer should accept that consequence in the brief rather than ask the printer to make it both unmistakable and residue-free.

For labels that need to be removed cleanly during normal use, compare the options in [permanent versus removable adhesive](/knowledge/permanent-vs-removable-adhesive/) before adding a security function.

## Retail, logistics and inspection workflow

In retail, the customer often sees the closure at the shelf or after purchase. The label should be positioned where the opening action is visible without instructions. In logistics, the inspector may be checking a carton seam or a pallet wrap; the label has to survive stacking, abrasion and temperature changes until that check.

A label can be intact and still unhelpful if nobody looks at it. Put the inspection point in the receiving or fulfilment procedure. Record what an intact seal looks like, what an interference mark looks like and who decides whether a package is quarantined. If the label is only checked after a customer complaint, it is collecting evidence too late.

## Regulatory requirements and the responsible party

Specific products may have mandatory tamper-evident features. For example, US rules for certain over-the-counter drug products address tamper-evident packaging, and the EU has rules for the safety features on certain medicinal products. These regimes are product-specific and can change; the label supplier should not be treated as the authority.

The brand or importer should identify the applicable requirement, the approved construction and the evidence the responsible person must keep. The printer can reproduce an approved label, advise on materials and run an application test. It cannot decide that a package satisfies a regulatory scheme or that a particular void pattern is accepted by a market.

## What to send for a tamper-evident quote

Send the actual container or a matching sample, the opening path, the surface material, the inspection point and the evidence you want to see. State whether the package is single-use or returnable, whether the label will be applied by hand or machine, and what residue is acceptable. Include the quantity, label size and any regulated requirement that has already been confirmed.

Ask for applied samples, not just swatches. Open one the intended way, try a deliberate peel and leave one in the real storage condition. Approve the construction against those observations. The printer can control print, die shape and material choice; the buyer controls the closure and the inspection process that gives the evidence meaning.
