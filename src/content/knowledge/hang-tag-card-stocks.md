A hang tag is handled more than a label. It is picked up, turned over, hung on a rail, taken off, put in a bag and sometimes kept. The card has to feel substantial enough for the brand and stiff enough to hang straight, but not so heavy that it cracks at the hole or refuses to sit flat.

That is why grammage is the first decision, not the artwork.

## What gsm actually measures

Gsm means grams per square metre. It is a weight, not a thickness, and two cards with the same gsm can feel different because of bulk, coating and finish. A 350 gsm coated card is usually about 0.35 mm thick; a 400 gsm board is roughly 0.42 mm. Kraft and recycled boards can be bulkier or softer at the same weight.

For a hang tag, the practical range is narrow:

| Stock | Feel | Best for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300 gsm coated | Light, flexible | Small accessories, short-run craft tags | Eyelet can tear out; tag can curl |
| 350 gsm coated | Firm, standard retail | Apparel, candles, accessories, most first orders | The default for a reason |
| 400 gsm coated | Heavy, premium | Foil, emboss, luxury and gift sets | Needs a larger hole clearance; costs more |
| 300–350 gsm kraft | Natural, matte | Coffee, soap, craft, sustainable positioning | Colour prints duller; shade varies |
| Recycled board | Textured, flecked | Natural brands, eco positioning | Batch shade variation; not always smooth enough for fine foil |

The table is a starting point, not a rule. A 300 gsm tag can be right for a jewellery brand that wants a delicate tag; a 400 gsm tag can be wrong for a small tag that will crack at the hole. The size and the hardware decide as much as the weight.

## Coated, uncoated and kraft

Coated card has a smooth surface that holds fine type and small logos. It prints colour predictably and takes a matte or gloss laminate cleanly. If the design has a photograph, a gradient or a small ingredient panel, coated is usually the safer choice.

Uncoated card has a tactile surface that absorbs ink. It looks more natural and takes a pen, which matters if the tag is used for handwritten pricing or a care note. The trade-off is that colour is less saturated and fine detail can fill in. A matte laminate can protect an uncoated look without changing it too much, but test the finish: some laminates make uncoated card look like coated card.

Kraft is an uncoated board with a visible fibre and a warm brown tone. It reads craft, coffee, soap or natural skincare. White ink on kraft can look chalky; a dark ink or a foil can look better. If the brand colour is critical, ask for a proof on the actual kraft, not on white coated card.

Recycled board is sold on its texture and its story. The shade can vary between batches, and the surface may not be smooth enough for a fine foil or a small reverse-out text. That variation is a property of the material, not a printing fault. If the design depends on a precise colour, recycled board is the wrong substrate.

## Grain direction and the hole

Card has a grain direction, like paper. A tag cut with the grain running vertically is stiffer along the hang; a tag cut across the grain can curl more. For a long, narrow tag, the grain direction affects how the tag hangs and how it survives handling. Ask the printer to keep the grain consistent across the run if the tag is large or the card is heavy.

The hole is where the card fails first. A 4 mm hole in 300 gsm card can ovalise after a few weeks; an eyelet spreads the load. A 400 gsm card is stiffer but more brittle at a tight radius, so the hole needs more clearance. The usual position is 8–10 mm from the top edge, centred, with about 3 mm of clear space around the hole.

If the tag will carry a heavy product or a long string, the hole and eyelet are part of the structural design, not a finishing detail.

## Which stock for which product

- **Apparel and accessories:** 350 gsm coated, matte laminate, eyelet. It survives the rail and feels substantial without being rigid.
- **Candles and home fragrance:** 350–400 gsm coated or textured uncoated; keep the warning text legible and leave room for a batch code.
- **Coffee, soap and craft:** 300–350 gsm kraft or recycled board; accept shade variation and choose ink colours that work on a brown sheet.
- **Luxury and gift sets:** 400 gsm coated with foil or emboss; budget for the die and the make-ready, and test the fold or hole.
- **Small jewellery tags:** 300–350 gsm; a 400 gsm tag can look out of scale and crack at a small hole.

## What this means for buyers

**Choose the weight with the hardware, not after it.** A 400 gsm tag needs a larger hole clearance and a stronger eyelet. A 300 gsm tag may need an eyelet to survive.

**Ask for the actual stock.** “350 gsm card” covers coated, uncoated, kraft and recycled boards with different bulk and print behaviour. Ask for the shade and surface you will receive.

**Do not use weight as a proxy for quality.** A clean cut, a centred hole and a tag that hangs straight matter more than 50 gsm.

**Test the finish on the stock.** Foil, emboss and white ink behave differently on coated, kraft and recycled board. A proof on the actual material is cheaper than a reprint.

## The card weight that tears at the hole before it looks worn

A heavy card can feel premium at the counter and fail at a small hole. The eyelet, string and product weight concentrate force at one point; if the hole clearance is too small or the card is brittle, the surface cracks or the eyelet pulls through. A lighter card may flex and ovalise instead. The failure begins at the attachment while the printed face still looks new.

Grain, coating and hardware alter how a given weight behaves. A heavy coated board can be less forgiving at a tight radius, while a lighter kraft sheet may need an eyelet simply to survive handling. Grain direction affects curl and stiffness along the hang. A folded header adds another stress line, and humid storage can soften the sheet before it reaches the rail. Weight alone does not tell you which construction will hold.

Use a hanging sample made from the actual stock, cut with the intended grain and hole. Hang it from the intended string with a representative product load, then inspect the hole, eyelet and curl rather than only the printed face. Request caliper and grain direction alongside the gsm figure. |---|---|---|
| Retail rail display | Stiffness along the hanging direction | Hang it from the production string |

| Small eyelet on a compact tag | Local clearance and brittleness | Check the card around the barrel |
| Folded or reinforced header | Crease strength at the score | Open and close the fold |
| Humid shop or stockroom | Curl and softening over time | Leave the sample in the environment |
