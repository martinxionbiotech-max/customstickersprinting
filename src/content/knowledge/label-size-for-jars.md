A jar has less straight body than a bottle of the same height: the shoulder curve starts early and the base has a radius. That is why a label sized from the jar's overall height creases at the top, and why the numbers below are smaller than buyers expect.

For the measurement method, see the [label size guide](/knowledge/label-size-guide/).

## Typical sizes by fill weight

| Jar size | Diameter | Body height | Label | Lid circle |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| 2 oz (60 ml) | 50 mm | 40 mm | 40 × 35 mm | 45 mm |
| 4 oz (120 ml) | 60 mm | 55 mm | 50 × 45 mm | 55 mm |
| 6 oz (180 ml) | 65 mm | 65 mm | 55 × 50 mm | 58 mm |
| 8 oz (240 ml) | 73 mm | 85 mm | 60 × 60 mm | 65 mm |
| 12 oz (360 ml) | 80 mm | 95 mm | 70 × 65 mm | 72 mm |
| 16 oz (480 ml) | 88 mm | 100 mm | 75 × 70 mm | 78 mm |
| 32 oz (950 ml) | 100 mm | 130 mm | 85 × 85 mm | 88 mm |

Lid circles assume 3–5 mm of visible lid around the label.

## Why the shoulder decides the height

The straight section of a jar body is shorter than it looks. On a typical 8 oz jar with an 85 mm body height, the shoulder curve starts about 12 mm below the rim and the base radius takes another 6 mm. That leaves roughly 67 mm of genuinely flat, vertical surface.

A 60 mm label fits comfortably. A 70 mm label reaches into the shoulder and lifts on one side, because the application cannot be perfectly square by hand. Subtract 6 mm from the measured body height before choosing, and the problem disappears.

## Jam, candle and cosmetic jars are not the same

| Jar type | What matters | Typical label |
|---|---|---|
| Jam and preserve | Condensation after filling, fridge storage | 60 × 60 mm, PP with chill adhesive |
| Candle | Glass warms to 50–60 °C, wax residue on the surface | 60 × 65 mm, heat-resistant adhesive |
| Cosmetic cream | Oil contact, bathroom humidity | 55 × 50 mm, PP with matte laminate |
| Honey | Sticky residue, slow pour | 65 × 55 mm, PP, oil-resistant adhesive |

Same jar shape, different adhesive and film. The size is similar; the specification is not.

## Wrap or panel on a jar?

Jars are wide relative to their height, so a full wrap covers a lot of glass and leaves little product visible. Most brands use one of three layouts:

1. **Front panel only** — 60–70% of the circumference, leaving glass visible at the sides. The classic preserve look.
2. **Full wrap** — for a strong colour block or a pattern. Circumference for a 73 mm jar is 229 mm; add 4 mm overlap.
3. **Front plus lid** — the label carries the brand and the lid carries the variant. Common on candles, where the lid is often the largest flat surface available.

A lid label is also the cheapest way to add a variant without reprinting the body label: one body label, three lid designs.

## What this means for buyers

**Measure the body, not the jar.** Total height includes the lid and the shoulder, and neither is printable.

**Decide whether the lid carries information.** If it does, size the body label for brand only and put the variant on the lid.

**Match the adhesive to the filling temperature.** A candle poured at 60 °C and a jam filled at 85 °C stress the adhesive differently from a cold-filled cosmetic.

**Keep 3–5 mm of lid visible.** A lid label that covers the lid completely looks like a mistake and hides the closure colour.

**Order the lid circles with the body labels.** A second run for a 60 mm circle costs more in setup than in labels.
