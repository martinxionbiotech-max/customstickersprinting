Beyond gloss and matte, a handful of finishes change how a label feels and catches light. They are applied after printing, they add a pass, and each has a failure mode worth knowing.

## The options

**Spot UV** — a glossy varnish applied to selected areas over a matte laminate. The contrast between matte and gloss creates a raised-looking highlight.

**Soft-touch laminate** — a velvety film with a rubberised feel. Premium in the hand; marks with oily fingers.

**Textured laminate** — a fine grain (linen, sand) that gives paper-like tactility to a film label.

**Varnish (overall)** — a thin protective coat, cheaper than laminate, less protective.

**Metallised stock** — a metallic film base rather than foil stamping; cheaper than foil for a full metallic look.

## Where each works

| Finish | Best on | Avoid on |
|---|---|---|
| Spot UV | Matte laminated labels, dark backgrounds | Uncoated paper (absorbs) |
| Soft-touch | Premium spirits, cosmetics, rigid boxes | Products handled with wet hands |
| Textured laminate | Craft and natural brands | Small labels (texture reads as noise) |
| Varnish | Short-life indoor labels | Anything wet or handled |
| Metallised stock | Full metallic backgrounds | Fine text over the metal |

## The cost logic

Every finish is an extra pass. Spot UV also needs a screen or plate for the pattern, which is a setup cost similar to a die — it pays at volume, not at 50 units.

Soft-touch and textured laminates are priced per square metre with a small setup; they are more accessible at low volume than spot UV.

Varnish is the cheapest protection and the weakest; it is not a substitute for laminate on a handled product.

## Failure modes

- **Spot UV on uncoated stock** — the varnish sinks in and the contrast disappears.
- **Soft-touch in a bathroom** — fingerprints and water marks become permanent.
- **Textured laminate on a 30 mm label** — the grain is larger than the design detail.
- **Varnish instead of laminate on a chilled product** — water reaches the ink and the label looks damp.

## What this means for buyers

**Choose the finish for the environment first, the look second.** Soft-touch is beautiful and unsuitable for anything handled wet.

**Spot UV is a volume technique.** At low quantities the setup dominates; consider a matte/gloss contrast in the print instead.

**Ask what the finish sits on.** A laminate under spot UV is part of the specification, not an optional extra.

**Request a physical proof.** Finish is the one property a PDF cannot show at all.
