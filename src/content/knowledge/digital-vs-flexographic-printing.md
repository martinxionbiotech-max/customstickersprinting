The reason most label printers quote in thousands is not policy. It is the economics of flexographic printing, which needs plates and make-ready before the first label is printed.

Understanding the two methods explains almost every price difference you will see.

## Flexographic printing

Flexo uses a flexible photopolymer plate wrapped around a cylinder. Ink transfers from an anilox roller to the plate, then to the substrate.

**Strengths**: very fast, cheap per unit at volume, excellent on long runs, handles a wide range of films.

**Weaknesses**: each colour needs a plate; each job needs make-ready and registration; changing artwork means changing plates.

Setup is typically several hundred dollars and a few hours. Spread over 100,000 labels it is negligible; spread over 50 it is absurd.

## Digital printing

Digital prints directly from a file — no plates. Setup is short, artwork changes are free, and a 50-unit run is a normal job.

**Strengths**: no plate cost, fast turnaround, variable data, economical below a few thousand units.

**Weaknesses**: higher cost per unit at volume, limited to digital-compatible stocks, colour matching to Pantone is approximate.

## Where the crossover sits

| Quantity | Better method | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 50–500 | Digital | Plate and make-ready dominate flexo |
| 500–2,000 | Digital, or flexo if artwork is stable | Depends on colours and stock |
| 2,000–10,000 | Flexo usually wins | Setup amortised |
| 10,000+ | Flexo | Per-unit cost dominates |

The exact crossover depends on the number of colours, the substrate and the finishing. A one-colour label on paper crosses earlier than a six-colour label on film.

## Why this matters for a first order

A 50-unit order exists because digital removed the plate cost. That is also why:

- **Artwork changes are cheap** at low volume — no plates to remake.
- **Variable data is possible** — each label can carry a different code.
- **Colour matching is approximate** — digital CMYK cannot hit every Pantone exactly.

## Colour in each method

Flexo prints spot colours mixed to a reference, which is how brands achieve an exact match across packaging. Digital approximates those colours with CMYK, usually closely enough for a label but not identically to a litho-printed carton.

If a brand colour must match existing packaging exactly, say so before the run and expect either a spot colour job or a physical proof to approve.

## What this means for buyers

**Low volume is a digital job, and that is why it costs more per unit.** The comparison is not "labels are expensive at 50" but "setup is spread over 50 instead of 50,000".

**Do not ask for exact Pantone matching on a 50-unit digital run.** Ask for a proof and approve the colour visually; exact matching is a flexo conversation.

**Reorder at the crossover.** Once a design sells steadily, moving from 50 to 2,000 changes the unit price materially and often moves the job to flexo.

**Ask which method is being quoted.** A price without a method is a number without a reason.
