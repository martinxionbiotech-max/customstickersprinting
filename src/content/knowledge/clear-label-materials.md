A clear label is a film, a print and a decision about opacity. Most of the cost and all of the visual result come from that third part.

## Why colour needs white

CMYK inks are translucent. Printed directly onto clear film and applied to a coloured product, they mix optically with whatever is behind them: a red logo on amber liquid reads brown, white text on dark glass disappears.

A white ink layer printed behind the design restores opacity. It is a separate pass, and it is the difference between a label that looks designed and one that looks like a sticker.

## Three white-ink strategies

| Strategy | Result | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Full flood | Entire label opaque, clear margin visible | Text-heavy labels, dark products |
| Spot white | Only logo and text opaque, rest transparent | The "floating" premium look |
| No white | Everything translucent | Subtle tints, deliberate effects |

Spot white needs a defined layer in the artwork. Without it, the printer guesses — and guessing produces either a flood (losing the effect) or an unreadable logo.

## Matching film to container

**Clear PP** is the flexible standard, good on curved bottles and squeeze containers.

**Clear PET** is stiffer and clearer, better for flat glass, spirits and premium cosmetics.

**Matte clear film** gives a frosted effect that hides bubbles and fingerprints — often the more forgiving choice on a hand-applied label.

## Where clear works, and where it does not

| Container | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Smooth clear glass | Best case |
| Frosted glass | Works if the frost is intended |
| Dark glass or plastic | Needs a white flood or it disappears |
| Textured or recycled board | Poor — film edges catch light |
| Kraft packaging | Looks like a mistake |

## Application quality

Clear film shows bubbles, dust and misalignment that white stock conceals. For runs above a few hundred on small containers, an applicator gives a visibly cleaner result; hand application needs a clean, dry surface and a squeegee from the centre out.

## What this means for buyers

**Decide the white areas in the artwork, not in the quote.** A clear label without a white-ink layer is a design decision that was never made.

**Clear is a premium, not a default.** It costs more than white film, and it only pays off on a container that suits it.

**Matte clear hides what gloss clear reveals.** On hand-applied labels, matte is usually the kinder choice.

**Ask for a sample on the actual container.** Transparent film on a white desk looks perfect; on amber glass it may not.
