Sticker thickness is quoted in microns and felt in the hand. It affects three things: how premium the sticker feels, how well it conforms to a curve, and how it survives peeling and abrasion.

## The typical range

| Construction | Total thickness | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Paper sticker | ~100–120 µm | Thin, tears easily |
| Standard vinyl | ~150–180 µm | The common default |
| Heavy vinyl | ~200–250 µm | Stiff, premium feel |
| Clear film | ~100–150 µm | Thin, invisible |
| Laminated vinyl | +20–40 µm | Adds scuff protection |

## What thickness buys

**Feel.** A 200 µm vinyl sticker feels substantial in the hand; a 100 µm paper sticker feels disposable. For merchandise and packaging inserts, that perception is part of the product.

**Peel strength.** Thicker film resists tearing during peeling, which matters for complex die cuts and for stickers removed by hand.

**Abrasion resistance.** More material means more to wear through — though lamination does more for surface protection than thickness does.

## What thickness costs

**Conformability.** A stiff film on a small radius (under ~30 mm) lifts at the edges. On a curved bottle or a narrow tube, thinner film wins.

**Edge visibility.** Thick film has a visible edge, which is fine on a matte product and wrong for a "no-label" look.

**Material cost.** Thicker stock costs more per square metre, and the difference compounds at volume.

## Thickness versus laminate

A 150 µm vinyl with a laminate usually outperforms a 250 µm vinyl without one, for less money. The laminate protects the surface; the face stock provides body.

If the goal is durability, laminate first. If the goal is feel, go thicker.

## What this means for buyers

**Do not buy thickness as a proxy for quality.** It is one property among several, and on curved surfaces it can be a liability.

**Laminate before thickening.** Scratch resistance comes from the film on top, not the bulk underneath.

**Match thickness to the surface.** Flat packaging can take heavy stock; small-radius bottles cannot.

**Ask what the total construction is.** Face stock, adhesive and laminate together — a single "microns" number usually means only the face.
