## The storage condition decides everything

A jam jar on a pantry shelf and the same jar in a fridge after opening are two different labelling problems. Food and beverage labels fail for physical reasons — moisture, cold, oil, heat — long before they fail for design reasons.

Start with the condition, not the artwork:

| Condition | Material | Adhesive |
|---|---|---|
| Ambient dry goods | Coated paper | Permanent acrylic |
| Chilled, condensation | PP | Chill-grade acrylic |
| Frozen (−18 °C) | PP or PET | Freezer-grade |
| Oily or greasy surface | PP | Oil-resistant |
| Hot-fill | PP | High-initial-tack |

A paper label on a chilled bottle will cockle within days, no matter how good the print is.

## Mandatory information

Food labels carry more required text than any other category: product name, net quantity, ingredients, allergens, storage instructions, best-before date, batch code, and the producer or importer. The requirements differ between the US, UK and EU, and they change.

Two practical consequences: design the label around the text rather than shrinking the text to fit, and leave a defined area for date and batch codes so they are not squeezed into the design later.

## Beverages: condensation and ice

Bottled drinks are the hardest case. A bottle goes from a warm shelf to an ice bucket to a table, and the label has to survive all three. PP with a chill-grade adhesive is the baseline; PET is better for immersion and for chemical resistance.

For clear bottles, a clear label with white ink gives the no-label look — but only if the design accounts for what sits behind it.

## Small runs and multiple SKUs

Food brands often launch with several flavours at low volume. Digital printing keeps the setup low, so a 50-unit run per flavour is viable, and the artwork can change between SKUs without new plates.

## What to send for a quote

The product, the storage condition, the container, the label size, the mandatory text length, quantity and destination. If the recipe or ingredient list is still changing, order a plain sample first to test adhesion before committing to print. Minimum 50 units.
