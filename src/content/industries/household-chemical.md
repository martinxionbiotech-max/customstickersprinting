A household chemical label has to survive the product, the container, the cleaning cupboard and the regulator's field list. Those are not separate jobs. A bottle of cleaner can look excellent on a shelf while the hazard text is crowded into an unreadable block or the label lifts from a low-surface-energy plastic after a spill.

This page describes production decisions for the pack. It is not legal advice. The classification, hazard statements, precautionary statements, translations and market approvals remain the responsibility of the brand, importer or supplier named on the label. Confirm the applicable rules with the authority or a qualified adviser and read the [disclaimer](/disclaimer/).

## The label is part of hazard communication

In the United States, OSHA's Hazard Communication Standard, [29 CFR 1910.1200](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1200), sets workplace label requirements for hazardous chemicals. In the European Union, [Regulation (EC) No 1272/2008 (CLP)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2008/1272/oj/eng) sets classification, labelling and packaging requirements for substances and mixtures. Both systems can require a product identifier, signal word, hazard statements, precautionary statements, pictograms and supplier information, with additional particulars in some cases.

The label designer should receive the approved content from the person responsible for classification, not build it from a generic template. Changes to a formula, concentration, fragrance or intended use can change the classification and the text. A label that was correct for the previous version is not automatically correct for the new one.

## GHS and CLP field pressure

The full hazard block can be long. On a small bottle, the product name and brand compete with statements that may need to remain legible at final size. The practical sequence is to place the approved mandatory block first, then the brand, then discretionary copy.

Do not solve a space shortage by shrinking hazard text below a readable size. A larger label, a multi-panel construction, a folded leaflet or a different container may be the honest answer. A printer can show how much area the supplied text consumes and flag a collision; it cannot decide that a statement can be shortened or omitted.

The US and EU are not the only destinations. A product sold in several markets may need separate versions or a combined label with language and field differences. Build a matrix before artwork so the language versions do not arrive one at a time near the print date.

## HDPE and PP are not forgiving surfaces

Many household products use HDPE or PP bottles because the material is tough and resistant to the contents. Those same plastics can be difficult for a pressure-sensitive adhesive. Surface energy is lower than glass, and mould release agents or surface contamination can reduce contact.

Ask for a sample on the actual bottle mould. Check the shoulder, the label band and any recessed panel. A label that sticks to a flat test panel may lift from a curved bottle. If the surface is especially difficult, discuss a suitable low-surface-energy adhesive or a surface treatment with the converter and container supplier.

Cleaning is part of adhesion. Dust, oil and condensation at the application line can form a weak layer under the label. Define the cleaning method and drying time, then keep the production line consistent with the sample test.

## Chemical resistance is a system property

A label is not chemically resistant because its face is called “chemical resistant”. The printed ink, varnish or laminate, face stock, adhesive and container all meet the product. A solvent that attacks the print can make a readable label illegible while the adhesive still holds. A surfactant that creeps under an edge can lift a label that resists a direct splash.

Test the finished label with the actual product. Apply it to the filled container, expose it to the spill, drip or wipe condition the pack may see, and inspect the edge and print. If the product is intended to be wiped with a household cleaner, test that cleaner too. Do not assume that water resistance predicts resistance to alcohol, acid, alkali or oil.

## Small containers and label geometry

Small bottles concentrate the problem. The printable area is narrow, the curvature is tight and the hazard block may be the largest element. A label that wraps too far around a small diameter can lift at the edge or cross a seam.

Measure the straight band, not the total bottle height. Keep the die-cut edge away from a sharp radius and leave room for the closure, the batch code and any child-resistant mechanism. A curved dieline may fit better than a rectangular label on a tapered bottle, but it must be drawn for the actual container.

If the mandatory text does not fit on the body, consider a multi-panel label or a leaflet. Check the market rules for the chosen format with the responsible party before designing the fold.

## Child-resistant packaging changes the closure

Where child-resistant packaging rules apply, the closure is part of the safety system, not just a cap. A label can interfere by covering the opening instructions, bridging the cap and body, or making the required squeeze-and-turn action harder to understand.

The label should not obstruct the closure's operation or any required tactile warning. If the label is also used to seal the pack, confirm the opening path with the closure supplier and test the complete package. US rules for child-resistant packaging sit within [16 CFR Part 1700](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-II/subchapter-E/part-1700); the EU has its own requirements under CLP and related product rules. Confirm the applicable scope with the importer or authority.

## Multi-language layouts without mixed-up text

A multilingual chemical label is a layout and version-control problem before it is a translation problem. Each language block should be supplied as approved text with a version identifier. The printer should not combine a new English block with an old French block because both happened to fit.

Set a column or panel order and keep it consistent across the range. Mark the language, market and label version on the artwork and on the approved proof. If the text length changes, reflow the whole block rather than reducing type until it fits. A language that uses a different script may need a font with complete glyph coverage and a print test at final size.

## Test durability before the production run

Run a small set of tests on the actual container. Apply the label as the line will apply it, then expose one sample to the product, one to the cleaning method, one to a wet wipe and one to the storage condition. Inspect the edge and print at defined intervals.

Do not treat a dry shelf sample as evidence for a bathroom or utility-room product. Condensation, steam, repeated handling and chemical drips can change the result. Keep the control sample and record the test condition so the next order can repeat the same specification.

| Specification decision | Why it matters for household chemicals | What to test |
|---|---|---|
| Face stock and laminate | Print and face may meet spills, solvents or abrasion | Real product, cleaning wipe and abrasion on the finished label |
| Adhesive and surface | HDPE and PP can resist standard adhesion | Actual bottle, cleaning method and application pressure |
| Label geometry | Small or curved containers concentrate edge peel | Die line, wrap, closure clearance and batch-code area |
| Mandatory text block | Hazard statements and languages consume space | Longest approved version at final size and legibility |
| Closure interaction | A label can obstruct a child-resistant action | Opening instructions, cap operation and seal path |
| Version control | Formula or market changes can change required text | Market, language, formula and artwork version matched |

## What to send for a chemical-label quote

Send the container material and a sample if possible, the label area, the approved text and language versions, the product's cleaning and spill exposure, the quantity and the destination. Include the closure type and whether the label must seal or stay clear of it. Tell us if the product is a regulated hazardous mixture so the quotation can account for the required text area.

The printer can reproduce approved content, recommend a construction and test attachment and print resistance. The brand or importer should confirm the classification, wording, translations and any child-resistant packaging requirement.
