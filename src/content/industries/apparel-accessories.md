Apparel is where a hang tag has to do the most work with the least space. It carries the brand, the size, the price, the material and sometimes a care instruction, and it is handled on a rail, in a fitting room and at the till. A tag that tears at the hole or curls at the corner makes the garment look cheaper than it is.

## The tag is part of the product

A clothing label is usually sewn in and almost invisible; a hang tag is the piece the buyer holds. That makes its physical qualities part of the brand: the weight of the card, the feel of the laminate, the colour of the eyelet and the length of the string. A 350 gsm tag with a clean cut and a centred hole reads more expensive than a 400 gsm tag with a ragged hole.

The first decision is the format. A 55 × 90 mm tag is a common apparel size because it fits a standard rail and leaves room for a logo, a product name and a small barcode. A 50 × 90 mm tag suits small accessories; a 60 × 100 mm tag suits outerwear, candles or gift sets. The size should follow the fixture and the text, not the other way round.

## Hole, eyelet and string

The hole is the weakest point. A 4 mm hole in 300 gsm card can ovalise after handling; an eyelet spreads the load and protects the card. Keep the hole 8–10 mm from the top edge, centred left to right, with about 3 mm of clear space around it. If the tag carries a heavy garment or a long string, the hole and eyelet are structural, not decorative.

String, waxed cord, elastic and safety pins each change the way the tag hangs. A knot adds 10–15 mm above the tag; a loop adds more. If the buyer specifies a finished drop, work backwards from the rail to the bottom of the tag, allowing for the knot. A tag that hangs too low can hide the product or catch on the rail.

## What the tag has to carry

A retail apparel tag often needs the brand, product name, size, colour, material, origin, care information and a barcode. That is a lot for a small card. Start with the mandatory text and the barcode, then design the brand around it. A barcode below a comfortable magnification may scan on a phone and fail at the till; if it does not fit, increase the tag rather than shrinking the symbol.

Care text can sit on the back or on a sewn-in label. If it sits on the hang tag, make sure it is legible at final size; a 4 pt care panel on a 350 gsm tag is a common cause of a reprint. The same applies to a material composition or country-of-origin statement.

## Logo stickers and packaging

A hang tag is often paired with a logo sticker on the mailer, tissue paper or poly bag. The sticker is a different specification: it goes on packaging, not on the garment, and it may be peeled or thrown away. A paper sticker is often adequate for a dry mailer; a vinyl sticker is better for a surface that will be handled or exposed to moisture.

Keep the two specifications separate. A logo sticker that uses the same artwork as the hang tag can look consistent, but the material and adhesive should follow the surface. A sticker that peels off a poly bag in transit is not a branding success.

## A first-order checklist

- Choose the card weight with the hole and eyelet, not after them.
- Decide the attachment and the finished drop before the dieline.
- Put the barcode and the mandatory text inside the safe area.
- Use an eyelet if the tag will be handled on a rail.
- Ask for a physical proof on the actual card; a screen cannot show the hole fit or the hang.
- Keep the logo sticker specification separate from the hang tag.

## What this means for buyers

**A hang tag is handled, not just read.** Specify the card, hole and attachment for the way the garment will be sold.

**A centred hole is worth more than 50 gsm.** A straight-hanging 350 gsm tag looks better than a crooked 400 gsm one.

**Do not shrink the barcode.** If it does not fit, change the tag format. A barcode that fails at the till costs more than a larger tag.

**Match the sticker to the surface.** The hang tag and the packaging sticker share a brand, not necessarily a material.
