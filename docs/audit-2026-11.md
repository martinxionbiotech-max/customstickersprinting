# Content and SEO audit — customstickersprinting.com

Date: 2026-11. Scope: local `dist` build (58 HTML pages, 35,878 words). The site is not yet deployed, so this audits the build that will go live, not a live crawl.

Method: `site-audit.py` for the structural metrics, plus per-page extraction (main content only, nav/footer stripped) for duplication, stance markers, FAQ length and external citation counts. Every number below comes from that run.

## 1. Keywords and long-tail coverage

### What the site targets today

| Layer | Pages | Query shape |
|---|---:|---|
| Product | 14 | Head terms: `bottle labels`, `clear stickers`, `die cut stickers` |
| Industry | 4 | Application: `labels for beauty brands`, `labels for candle brands` |
| Knowledge | 25 | Informational: `paper vs pp labels`, `die cut vs kiss cut`, `what is moq` |
| Service | 2 | `low moq label printing`, `small batch sticker printing` |

Titles are all inside 60 characters, unique, and none duplicate a canonical. That part is clean.

The problem is the product layer. Every product title follows the same two-word pattern plus brand:

```
Bottle Labels | Custom Stickers Printing          (40)
Clear Labels | Custom Stickers Printing           (39)
Die Cut Stickers | Custom Stickers Printing       (43)
```

Fourteen pages, one pattern, zero modifiers. Those head terms are contested by printers with decades of domain authority, paid links and thousands of pages. A 58-page site will not rank for `custom labels` on merit; it can rank for `waterproof labels for 8 oz bottles` because nobody has written that page properly.

### Long-tail clusters with no page

| Cluster | Example query | Commercial value | Page today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Label sizing | `what size label for 250 ml bottle` | High — pre-purchase | None |
| Cost | `how much do 500 custom labels cost` | Highest | One KB article, no numbers |
| Marketplace compliance | `amazon fnsku labels`, `suffocation warning labels` | High B2B | None |
| Application pages | `hot sauce bottle labels`, `honey jar labels`, `coffee bag labels` | High | Only 4 broad industries |
| Format decision | `roll labels vs sheet labels` | Medium | Partial in KB |
| Barcode | `upc label printing requirements` | Medium | None |
| Templates | `label template 2.5 x 3 inch` | Top-funnel magnet | None |
| Surface-specific | `labels for hdpe bottles`, `labels for powder coated tins` | Medium | KB only |
| Cold chain | `freezer labels that stay stuck` | Medium | KB only |
| Packaging stickers | `thank you stickers`, `seal stickers`, `mailer stickers` | Medium | None |

Two service pages (`/low-moq-label-printing/`, `/small-batch-sticker-printing/`) overlap in intent — low minimum labels versus low minimum stickers. Text overlap is only 4.8%, so they are not duplicates, but both are orphans and both chase the same "small quantity" searcher. Keep both, link both, and differentiate them explicitly by format.

### Keyword judgement

1. **Rewrite the 14 product titles with a modifier that matches a real query.** `Bottle Labels — Waterproof Stock, 50 Unit Minimum` beats `Bottle Labels`. The H1 can stay short.
2. **The three highest-value missing pages are size guide, cost guide, and Amazon/FBA labels.** They sit at the moment of purchase, not the moment of research.
3. **Application pages beat generic industry pages.** "Labels for beauty brands" is broad; "labels for 30 ml serum bottles" is a query with a buyer behind it.

## 2. AIO (AI answer optimisation)

| Item | Status |
|---|---|
| `llms.txt` | Missing |
| Markdown sidecars (`.md` twins) | 0 of 58 |
| External citations | 0 across 35,878 words |
| FAQPage schema | 16 pages, 61 questions, median answer 146 characters |
| Article schema | 25 knowledge pages |
| Service schema | 14, but `availability: InStock` with no price |
| AI crawler rules in robots.txt | Present: GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended |
| Definitional blocks | None — no glossary, no one-sentence definitions |
| Author entity | None — Organization only, no named person |

The robots file invites AI crawlers, and the content is written in exactly the shape that gets quoted (short answer, table, mechanism, caveat). Then nothing machine-readable is exposed. This is the largest single gap on the site and the cheapest to close.

Three fixes: generate `llms.txt` from the collections at build time; emit a `.md` twin per page with `<link rel="alternate" type="text/markdown">`; add two or three verified external references per knowledge article (FINAT test methods, ISO 12647-6 for flexo process control, 21 CFR 175.105 for food-contact adhesives, ISO 4892-2 for weathering, ISO/IEC 15416 for barcode grading).

The `Offer` node needs fixing regardless: `availability: InStock` with no price is a claim the site cannot support. Remove `availability`, keep the quote-based description.

## 3. Depth and breadth

| Section | Pages | Words (median) | Tables |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Knowledge | 25 | 621 | 24 |
| Products | 14 | 820 | 26 |
| Industries | 5 | 493 | 3 |
| Conversion (order, quote, contact, faq) | 4 | 400 | 2 |
| Support (about, shipping, artwork, privacy, terms) | 5 | 380 | 2 |

Depth per article is respectable for a reference library and thin for a cornerstone. There is no page that is unmistakably the best resource on its topic — nothing above 1,200 words, no worked example, no diagram.

Five pages are under 400 words and all five are commercial: `/industries/` (182), `/request-a-quote/` (196), `/contact/` (200), `/products/` (383), `/small-batch-sticker-printing/` (385). The request-a-quote page in particular is the end of the funnel and carries less text than a blog intro.

Breadth is uneven in a specific way: 25 articles answer "which material / how does it work", while nothing answers "what size do I need / what will it cost / how fast can I get it". That is the difference between a library and a shop.

## 4. Reader value

Strong:

- Every product page states who it is for and where it fails, not just what it is.
- Every knowledge article ends with "what this means for buyers" — four decisions, not a summary. This is the part competitors do not write.
- Tables carry the actual trade-off (material × condition, quantity × method), so a reader can decide without reading prose.
- No fabricated numbers anywhere: no fake case studies, no invented certifications, no made-up prices.

Weak:

- **No prices, no ranges, no worked example.** "Quoted per project" is honest but it is also the answer to the first question a buyer asks. A page explaining what drives cost, with a worked arithmetic example clearly labelled as illustrative, would serve readers better than silence.
- **No images at all.** This is a visual product. A bleed diagram, a die-cut versus kiss-cut diagram and a roll anatomy diagram would each add more than 500 words.
- **No author.** 382 words on the about page, no name, no location, no company registration. For a site asking strangers to wire money for a first order, that is a trust gap.
- **Same skeleton in 25 articles.** Short answer table → mechanism → failure modes → buyer implications. By the fourth article a reader can predict the structure, which makes original content feel templated.

## 5. Editorial stance

Measured: 4 of 25 knowledge articles contain an explicit stance marker ("we would", "our view", "in our experience", "the mistake"). The other 21 make judgements in a neutral voice — "Paper is the wrong answer regardless of price" is a strong claim with no one behind it.

This matters for two reasons. Readers trust attributed judgement more than unattributed fact, and AI engines preferentially quote sources that take a clear position with a reason attached.

The fix is small and high-yield: a 60–80 word "Our view" block per article, first person, one recommendation, one reason, one exception. Twenty-five of those is roughly 1,800 words and it changes how the whole site reads.

## 6. Originality

| Set | Pairs over 12% 5-gram Jaccard | Highest |
|---|---:|---:|
| Knowledge base (25 articles) | 1 | 12.6% |
| Product pages (14) | 46 | 19.8% |

The knowledge base is genuinely original. One pair crosses the threshold and only just (`production-lead-time` versus `what-is-moq`, both about fixed setup cost).

Product page similarity is template floor, not copied content: the shared sentences are the CTA block and the ordering specification, repeated on all 14 pages by design. The body of each product page is distinct.

No AI-assembly traces: no `utm_source=chatgpt.com` in any link, no mixed-language anchors, no boilerplate tails appended to descriptions to hit a length.

## 7. The three things costing the most points

1. **AIO is empty (30/100, weight 15%).** 35,878 words with zero citations and no machine-readable entry point. Cheapest large gain on the site.
2. **No attributed stance (20/100, weight 10%).** Original thinking with nobody's name on it. Small edit, disproportionate effect on both trust and quotability.
3. **Descriptions and product titles (SEO 74/100, weight 20%).** Thirty descriptions sit at 120–139 characters; fourteen product titles are head terms with no modifier. Mechanical to fix.

## 8. Fix list, ranked by weighted gain

| # | Action | Dimension | Gain | Est. total |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | `llms.txt`, 58 markdown sidecars, 2–3 verified citations per article, definitional blocks | AIO 30 → 88 | +8.7 | 73.9 |
| 2 | "Our view" block on all 25 articles | Stance 20 → 85 | +6.5 | 80.4 |
| 3 | 14 product titles with modifiers; 30 descriptions to 140–155; 5 page FAQs | SEO 74 → 90 | +3.2 | 83.6 |
| 4 | Deepen the five thin pages; build 3 cornerstones (size guide, cost guide, FBA labels) | Depth 55 → 78 | +2.3 | 85.9 |
| 5 | Fix 3 orphans; add refund and cookies pages; authored about page | Completeness 82 → 93 | +1.65 | 87.6 |
| 6 | Remove `availability` from Offer; align `areaServed` with "worldwide" | Consistency 88 → 96 | +1.2 | 88.8 |
| 7 | Vary article formats (teardown, checklist, decision tree) | Originality 84 → 87 | +0.45 | 89.2 |

Overlap between items 1 and 2 is real — both touch every article — so a realistic post-fix score is **86–88**, not 89.

## 9. What I would not do

- **Do not add more knowledge articles before fixing stance and citations.** Twenty-five is enough breadth; the problem is not quantity.
- **Do not chase `custom labels`.** The head term is unwinnable at this size and the traffic is low-intent anyway.
- **Do not invent prices, case studies or certifications to fill the gaps.** The "quoted per project" position is defensible; a fake "from $0.12" is not.
- **Do not merge the two low-MOQ service pages.** They target different formats and overlap only 4.8%. Link them instead.

| Principal | Wang Wei | About, contact, schema `founder` |

The About page went from 382 to 819 words and now includes a "how to verify us" section linking to the Chinese company registry and the D-U-N-S lookup, plus a payment-fraud warning.

## What is deliberately not published

The bank account number. It appears nowhere in the build — verified by scanning all 59 pages for the digits. Invoices are issued in the legal entity's name and carry the banking details; publishing an account number on a public site invites invoice-fraud clones and gives a stranger a target.

## Effect

- Organisation schema now carries `legalName`, two `identifier` values, a city-level address and a named founder, which is what Google's entity reconciliation looks for.
- `areaServed` was corrected from US/UK/EU only to include Worldwide, matching the site copy.
- The wrong region string ("Hebei, China") was removed from the contact page and the config.
